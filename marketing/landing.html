Monthly budgets break when you're paid weekly.

Months have three paychecks, bills land between pay dates, and a calendar grid hides what's actually left before the next deposit. Expense Tracker starts from the week instead.

The usual way

One big monthly number. A bill due on the 3rd and a paycheck on the 5th make the month look worse than it is, and "left to spend" is a guess.

Pay-week budgeting

Each bill sits in the Friday-ending week it's actually due. The month is just those weeks added up, so you always know what's left to collect and what's left to pay.

Questions

Is my financial data private?

Yes. There's no account and no server. Everything is stored locally on your device, and nothing is ever uploaded.

Is this a subscription?

No. It's a one-time purchase.

Do I need to be paid weekly?

No, any schedule works. It's organized around Friday-ending weeks because that's when most paychecks and bills land, however often you're paid.

Can I use it on more than one computer?

Yes. Your data lives on each computer separately (there's no cloud sync), so use the built-in Export and Import backup to move your budget between them.

Does it connect to my bank?

No, by design. You enter your balances yourself, which takes seconds a week and keeps your bank credentials out of the picture entirely.

Is there a phone app?

Not yet. It's a desktop app for Windows, macOS (Apple Silicon) and Linux.

My computer shows a security warning when I install it.

The installers aren't code-signed yet, so Windows SmartScreen or macOS Gatekeeper may warn you the first time. On Windows choose "More info" then "Run anyway". On a Mac, right-click the app, choose Open, and confirm. It's a one-time step.
